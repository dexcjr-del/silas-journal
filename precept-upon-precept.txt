*Dexter Carter · One verse a day · All Scripture from the ESV*

**TL;DR:** The most important verse in the Bible about *how to read the Bible* started as a drunk joke. A room full of staggering priests used it to mock the prophet — and God overheard, picked up their punchline, and made it His entire teaching method. Here's what they said, why scholars are confident that's what's happening, and what it changes about your mornings.

---

## The scene

It's around 730 BC. The northern kingdom is rotting from the inside, and Assyria is massing on the horizon like a storm you can see coming for years. Into this mess walks Isaiah with a message nobody wants.

Now read Is 28:7 slowly: "These also reel with wine and stagger with strong drink; the priest and the prophet reel with strong drink, they are swallowed by wine, they stagger with strong drink, they reel in vision, they stumble in giving judgment."

The *priest* and the *prophet*. Drunk. At work.

Then verse 9 — and most commentators agree these are the mockers' voices, not Isaiah's: "To whom will he teach knowledge, and to whom will he explain the message? Those who are weaned from the milk, those taken from the breast?" Translation: *who does he think he's talking to — babies?*

Then verse 10, the impression: *"Tsav la-tsav, tsav la-tsav! Qav la-qav, qav la-qav!"* Precept upon precept, line upon line. High, mocking, sing-song. The whole room laughs.

They thought they were burying him. They were writing his thesis statement. And God does something wild with their mockery in verse 13 — He repeats their words back and adds that to those who refuse it, this same word becomes a snare: "that they may go, and fall backward, and be broken, and snared, and taken."

## The man

Isaiah isn't some wild-eyed desert hermit. He's a statesman — he walks the halls of kings, advises Ahaz and Hezekiah, and once preached naked and barefoot for three years as a walking object lesson (Is 20 — it's in there). He's the prophet who saw the throne room in chapter 6 and spent the rest of his life trying to get the words out. He'll tell a king he's a fool and tell a drunk priest he's drunk, and somehow both of them listen.

This is the man they laughed at.

## The verse

> "For it is precept upon precept, precept upon precept, line upon line, line upon line, here a little, there a little." — Is 28:10

The Hebrew: *tsav la-tsav* — command upon command. *Qav la-qav* — a *qav* is a builder's measuring line, the snapped string that keeps a wall straight. *Ze'ir sham, ze'ir sham* — here a little, there a little.

## The cross-references

- **Dt 6:7** — teach them "when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise." Not in a weekend seminar. In the walking around. Here a little, there a little.
- **2Tm 2:15** — "rightly handling the word of truth." The Greek is *orthotomeo*: to cut straight, like a builder's line. There's that *qav* again, fifteen centuries later.
- **Heb 5:14** — "solid food is for the mature, for those who have their powers of discernment trained by constant practice." Practice. Reps. Small bites, over time.

## What it means, and what it means for you

God never asked you to swallow the Bible whole. A wall isn't built by dropping a house on a foundation — it's built line upon line. Snap the string, lay the course, check it straight, lay the next one.

That's the whole program here. One verse a day. The words underneath it. What it meant, what it means, what it asks of you. Five minutes, before your coffee cools.
